The field line of C4

The Earth and its dipole field, seen from the side: the played key's field line, the lines of every C for scale, and the plasmasphere, shaded, out to the plasmapause. A stroke at one foot sends its crack up the line as a spread of frequencies, coloured from the nose (the fastest, bright) down to the low ones (slow, amber), which you can watch fall behind; at the far foot each echo turns back. Chorus sparks at the equator.

Spectrogram

Each whistler is a falling curve that starts at its nose, the note, with a short rising branch above it that stops at the duct's cutoff, half the gyrofrequency. Its echoes fall more slowly, and the crack's tweeks hook down onto the waveguide's cutoff near 1.8 kHz.
