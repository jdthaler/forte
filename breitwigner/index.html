Lines

Every ringing key's Breit–Wigner line, drawn at its mass: as high as the chance |ψ|² that the state is still there, as wide as its width Γ. Dashed, the last key's line whole, and at the right the same line under a glass, four widths to either side. A line sinks without changing its shape. Let a key go and a second channel opens: its line spreads as it falls.

Decay law

|ψ|² of each key · dashed: e^(−t/τ)
The chance that each state is still there, on a logarithmic scale against time (now at the right). An exponential decay is a straight line, and its slope is the width: a factor e every lifetime τ = 1/Γ. Where a line bends down, a channel has opened.

Amplitude

The last key's amplitude ψ in the complex plane, in slow motion: it turns once for every cycle of the note and shrinks by e^(−πΓ/M) each turn, so a wide state spirals in within a few turns and a narrow one circles for thousands. What is heard is its height, the imaginary part (the bar at the right).

Spectrum

A narrow state is a spike here and a wide one a hump: the line itself, once the note has had its lifetime to sound.
