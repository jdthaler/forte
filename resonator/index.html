The strings of C4 under the coil

The key's first string, its motion in slow motion and hugely exaggerated, from the engine's partials. The coil over it lights up with its current; the hammer strikes at the left, an eighth of the way along; the bridge is at the right. A partial whose node sits under the coil cannot be driven.

Swell and lock

The last eight seconds of the focused note: the coil current's peak (gold), the string's swing under the coil (blue, in dB) and how far the phase-locked loop has pulled its oscillator from the partial it drives (green, in cents). A bass string swells for seconds; a treble string within a fraction of one.

Spectrogram

live output · ticks: the spectrum's markers
A struck note thins from the top down. A driven note does the opposite: it grows from nothing, and holds its partials as long as the key is down. The partials sit a little sharp of the harmonics (the wire's stiffness), which is why a rich drive never quite locks its upper harmonics.
