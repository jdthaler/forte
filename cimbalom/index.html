How the note dies away

The last key's force on the bridge, in decibels, over the last eight seconds. Its strings start in step and drain into the bridge together, fast; as their small mistuning pulls them apart they stop driving it together, and the sound falls slower or beats. Perfect unisons give one straight line; a wide spread gives beats.

The last stroke

The force between the hammer and the strings, against time. Cotton stays on a millisecond or more and rises gently, so it leaves the upper partials out; bare wood jumps at once. A light hammer is thrown back early, often by the wave that returns from the near bridge, and a whippy stick can drive the head back for a second blow.
