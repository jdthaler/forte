The cistern and the pumps

play a key
The pumpers push air into the bronze bowl (the pnigeus) standing upside down in the water. The air pushes the water down inside and up outside: the difference in height is the wind. When the water inside reaches the rim, air bubbles out under it, so the bowl holds the wind up to that and no more.

The wind

The last eight seconds. Each stroke lifts the wind, and the water under the rim bounces on the air after it; a chord draws it down; when the pumpers fall behind it sags until they catch up.

The pipes on their slides G4

The four ranks, one behind another, longest pipes at the left, drawn to scale. A key pushes its slide in under all four; the ranks whose stopcocks are open sound.
