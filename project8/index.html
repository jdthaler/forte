The receiver

frequency against time, as Project 8 plots it
Each electron as Project 8's receiver sees it: its frequency in the real trap, in MHz above the reference (the krypton line, or tritium's endpoint), against time. A track climbs as the electron radiates and cools, about 300 MHz a second; a collision with the gas makes it jump (a dot) or ends it (a cross), and a track that reaches the trap's edge as its pitch angle closes ends too.

The trap of C4

The magnetic bottle: the field along the axis, B₀ (1 + z²/L²), rising to the walls. Each electron bounces between the points where the field turns it back, here slowed to the note's scale (the real bounce is a hundred million times a second); one that bounces further sees more field, so it sounds a little sharp, and its Doppler vibrato is deeper. As an electron cools its bounce widens, until it reaches the walls and leaves.

Electron energies

the electrons played, and tritium's β spectrum
How many electrons of each energy you have played, over tritium's β spectrum (the curve) with its endpoint, 18.57 keV, and the krypton line, 17.82 keV. Project 8 measures this spectrum by frequency: a neutrino's mass would pull the endpoint in and bend the last few eV of the curve.
